How the model works

A frozen-encoder, late-fusion regressor, wrapped in a retraining pipeline.

What it predicts

The target is the log of a video's views minus the log of the channel's trailing average, log(1+views) − log(1+trailing_avg). The trailing average is the mean of the channel's five previous uploads, so a video never sees itself. Positive means it beat the channel's usual, negative means it fell short. The predicted value is converted back to an expected view count.

Only the three small branches and the head are trained. Embeddings are computed once and stored in Postgres (pgvector), so training takes minutes on a CPU.

Training setup

ChoiceValue
Loss and optimizerHuber loss, Adam, learning rate 2e-5, weight decay 1e-4
RegularizationDropout 0.2, Gaussian noise (0.02) on embeddings, early stopping with patience 10
SplitChronological 80/10/10 by publish date, so the model is tested on newer videos
ScalingScalers are fitted on the training split only, to avoid leakage
DataEnglish-language personality, gaming and commentary channels, 2025 onwards, Shorts excluded

Retraining and promotion

An Airflow pipeline ingests new videos from the YouTube API, embeds them, retrains, and checks for drift. A new model replaces the live one only if it passes two gates:

Hard gate: it must beat a simple linear baseline on the trailing average.

Soft gate: a paired bootstrap on the difference in Spearman correlation against the live model. It is rejected only if it is confidently worse, so ties go to the model trained on newer data.

Serving

FastAPI loads one exported bundle (weights, scalers, column order, encoder config) and a test checks that serving reproduces the training path. It runs on an Oracle Cloud VM behind Caddy, with a Next.js front end on Vercel and per-IP rate limiting.
