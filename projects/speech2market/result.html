Model Performance Summary

The predictive performance of the proposed macro-sentiment framework was evaluated across three financial instruments: S&P 500 Index (SPX), Gold Futures (GOLD), and the CBOE Volatility Index (VIX). The 10-Year U.S. Treasury Yield (TNX) is reported for completeness but was excluded from significance testing because its signal was judged too noisy during development to draw reliable conclusions from.

Embeddings Ablation: Does Speech Content Actually Help?

The full two-stage architecture was fit twice per target, once with Fed speech embeddings included as features, once without, while holding everything else identical (search space, purge logic, sample weighting). A paired bootstrap (n = 2,000 resamples, 90% confidence interval, 334 held-out test rows) tests whether the difference between the two is distinguishable from noise. Each line below spans the 90% CI of (no-embeddings score − with-embeddings score); the dot marks the observed difference. A dashed line at zero marks "no difference." Indigo = embeddings significantly better. Red = embeddings significantly worse. Gray = not statistically significant at this sample size (line crosses zero).

For AUC/F1, a line entirely left of zero (indigo) means embeddings win. For RMSE, a line entirely right of zero (indigo) means embeddings win, since lower RMSE is better, so the sign convention flips for that panel.

Out of 9 target × metric batteries tested significant, SPX t+30 is the only target where all three metrics agree, and is therefore the strongest, most internally consistent evidence in this study that embeddings carry real signal. Everything else is a single metric out of three, which is close to the false-positive rate you'd expect from testing this many comparisons at a 90% confidence level without correction (worth reading as suggestive rather than conclusive). VIX t+30 is a genuine negative result in the opposite direction: embeddings measurably hurt there.

Final Conclusion

Central bank speech embeddings do not provide a uniform edge across every asset and horizon. The evidence for a genuine effect is narrow and asset/horizon-specific: a clean, three-metric-significant improvement for SPX at the 30-day horizon, a handful of single-metric improvements for GOLD and VIX at shorter horizons, and a significant negative effect for VIX at 30 days. Most target/metric combinations show no statistically distinguishable difference. TNX did not clear the bar for inclusion in significance testing at all. The honest read is that Fed speech content carries some real, situational information, most convincingly for medium-term equity positioning rather than a broad, dependable signal across every market this study touched.

Disclaimer

Despite the findings that The FED's speeches do not carry uniformly meaningful signals across multiple horizons through multiple asset classes, this ML model genuinely carries edge (low RMSE, high directional accuracy) before considering about slippage, transaction fees, etc.

Marcos López de Prado, in Advances in Financial Machine Learning, argues that models claiming very high out-of-the-box AUC (roughly above 0.70) on directional price forecasts are usually a red flag for data leakage or look-ahead bias, not genuine skill. Read in that light, the mid-range AUCs reported here are more plausible as a real, if modest and narrow, signal than a suspiciously high one would be.

A Note on Inference-Time Sign Flips

Not every prediction will make intuitive sense in isolation. A speech read as bearish by the Fed can still be followed by positive returns across multiple asset classes and horizons, and vice versa. This isn't necessarily a model failure. Markets are largely efficient, meaning most available information is already priced in before a statement becomes official. During downturns, even overtly bearish commentary can trigger a relief rally simply because the worst was already priced in weeks earlier. During upturns, even bullish commentary can trigger selling, since good news has already been priced in and it becomes a convenient point for profit-taking.

This dynamic is consistent with Eugene Fama's Efficient Market Hypothesis and Andrew Lo's Adaptive Market Hypothesis, both of which help explain why markets are so difficult to predict from news sentiment alone. The relationship between "news tone" and "price direction" is regime-dependent rather than fixed.
