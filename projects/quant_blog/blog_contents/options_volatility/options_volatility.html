The Engine Room

To build the surface, we don't just "plot data." We have to reverse-engineer the market's expectations using the Black-Scholes inversion. It's like un-baking a cake to find out exactly how much sugar (volatility) was used.

Black-Scholes Inversion

$$ C_{mkt} - C_{BS}(\sigma) = 0 $$

We aren't solving for price. We know the price. We solve for $\sigma$ (Implied Volatility).

Brent's Method: Standard Newton-Raphson is too unstable for the jagged reality of OTM options. Brent's method guarantees convergence without derivatives.

Vectorization

$$ \mathbf{V} = f(\mathbf{S}, \mathbf{K}, \mathbf{T}) $$

Processing 2,913 contracts individually is slow.

NumPy: We vectorized the entire chain. No `for` loops. The entire surface calculates in milliseconds, allowing real-time risk assessment.

Cubic Spline Interpolation

Market data is discrete and noisy. The world is continuous.

We used scipy.interpolate.griddata with the cubic method to fit a smooth manifold over the scattered strike/expiry points. This reveals the hidden topology between the trades.

Human Readability

The Black-Scholes equation thinks in Years ($T=1.0$). Humans think in Days ($T=365$).

Our dashboard converts the $T$ parameter back to "Days to Expiration" for the visualization. The math doesn't care, but your brain does.
